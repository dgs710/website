Phase 01

Image analysis

Every raw field is focus-selected, segmented crystal-by-crystal, and measured.

Phase 02

Aggregation & melting temperature

The three ROIs collapse into one melting curve per well; a Boltzmann fit gives Tm, and empty wells are caught automatically.

Phase 03

Population analysis

Across the plate, melting points are ranked and their response to denaturant concentration is quantified in °C per molar.

OWL — Overleaf → Word

Written your paper in Overleaf but your advisor needs Word? Upload the LaTeX sources, pick a mode, download an editable .docx — fonts, figures, equations and ACS citations carried across.

Live — opens in a new tab

The OWL converter runs as its own small web app. Click below to open it, enter the access password, then upload your Overleaf source .zip, pick a mode, and download the Word document.

Launch OWL →
Two modes

Pick what the reader needs

Both take the same upload — your .tex, references.bib and figures (or the whole Overleaf source .zip).

Editable Word

Times New Roman, justified, title page, running header, Arabic body / Roman appendix numbering, real Word equations, and clickable ACS citations built straight from your .bib. Best when the reader needs to edit or comment.

Editable + live Zotero

The same document, but citations come out as Zotero RTF/ODF-Scan markers {Author, Year} plus a {Bibliography} placeholder — one scan turns them into live, editable Zotero fields. Best when the reader manages references in Zotero.

The workflow

Four steps, a few seconds

1

Download from Overleaf

Menu → Download → Source (a .zip with your .tex, .bib and figures).

2

Upload it to OWL

Drop the .zip in — OWL finds the main .tex and its bibliography automatically.

3

Pick a mode

Editable Word, or Editable + live Zotero citations.

4

Download the Word doc

Hand main.docx to your advisor. Done.

Zotero mode

Make citations live — one-time scan

Only needed if you chose the Zotero mode and want editable Zotero fields.

  1. Import your .bib into Zotero (File → Import) — the same file Overleaf syncs.
  2. Open the downloaded .docx in LibreOffice and Save As .odt.
  3. Zotero → Tools → RTF/ODF Scan → “ODF (to ODF)”, pick the .odt.
  4. Confirm each source once in the dialog (resolves any ambiguous author + year).
  5. Open the result — every citation is a live Zotero field and the bibliography is generated at the {Bibliography} marker.
Editable is close, not pixel-identical. Word re-wraps text with its own line-breaking engine while LaTeX uses Knuth–Plass, the “Times” fonts differ slightly, and Word lacks LaTeX hyphenation — so line and page breaks drift. That's a property of the two systems, not a setting. If a reader needs it to look exactly like the PDF, just hand them the compiled Overleaf PDF; OWL is the editable hand-off.
